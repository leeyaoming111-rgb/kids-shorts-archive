# Batch 01: Tumble & Pip, Fables About Friendship

- **status:** approved by the owner 2026-10-12 (see "Owner decisions" at the end). Next: Fable 1 as the pipeline test.
- **series:** Tumble & Pip
- **output:** 10 standalone Shorts (9:16, about 45 s each) and 1 long-form episode (16:9, about 9 min, past the 8-minute mid-roll ad mark) made from the same scenes
- **audience:** ages 3 to 5, Made for Kids, UK English captions
- **lesson family:** social-emotional learning (SEL): helping, sharing, honesty, being kind
- **sources:** Aesop's fables only (public domain). Retold from the plots, never from any modern book, film or song version.

## Why this batch

- Fables give each Short a tested story shape: a want, a problem, a twist and a clear moral.
- Tumble (a big, eager fox cub) and Pip (a tiny, clever bird) map straight onto the classic animal roles, and Tumble already is a fox.
- Most scenes reuse the Picnic Meadow set from Episode 2 (apple tree, big stump, stream, basket, mushrooms) and Bo the turtle from Lulu & Bo, so new modelling stays small.
- Every Short ends on the same moral chorus, which makes the 10 Shorts feel like one series and gives the episode a ready-made recap.

## Adaptation rules (apply to every fable)

1. **Plot from Aesop, everything else ours.** Our characters, gags, dialogue, song, setting and twist. Do not copy any modern retelling's wording, character designs, scenes or songs.
2. **Soften for ages 3 to 5.** No eating, starving, injury or real danger. Keep the lesson and replace any punishment with a gentle consequence and a kind fix.
3. **Tumble and Pip are always friends.** Fables with a mean character get a misunderstanding, a show-off moment or a silly mistake instead. Nobody is a villain.
4. **One idea per Short.** One moral, said once in the story and once in the chorus.
5. **Each Short stands alone.** No "part 2". A child who sees only one Short gets the whole story.

## The 10 fables

Every Short uses the same 45 s skeleton so the episode can join them cleanly:

| Part | Time | Short version | Episode version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0.0-3.0 | the problem in one image plus a question line | same |
| Sting | 3.0-5.0 | "Tumble and Pip!" | removed (played once at the episode start) |

| Problem and tries | 14.0-26.0 | gag with setup, anticipation and payoff | same |

### 1. The Lion and the Mouse: "Tiny Pip, Big Help"

- **Hook (0-3 s):** Tumble wrapped head to tail in his own kite string, eyes peeking out. "Uh-oh! Who can help?"
- **Story:** Tumble flies his kite and tells Pip, kindly but wrongly, "You're too tiny to help with a big kite!" A gust spins the kite round him and he ends up wrapped like a parcel and toppling over. Pip flies down and pecks the knot, peck, peck, peck. The string springs loose and Tumble rolls out.
- **Gag:** Tumble wriggles, hops and rolls while tied up, then the unwinding spins him like a top.
- **Moral:** Tumble: "Tiny friends can be BIG helpers!"
- **Assets:** kite from Episode 1, string as a spline tube, meadow.

### 2. The Fox and the Grapes: "Sour Apples?"

- **Hook:** Tumble at full stretch on tiptoe under a red apple that is just out of reach. "So close!"
- **Story:** Tumble jumps, jumps and jumps again and misses every time. He crosses his arms: "Those apples are probably sour anyway!" Pip lands on his nose: "Or... we could try together?" Tumble climbs onto the stump, Pip tugs the stem, and the apple drops into Tumble's paws. One bite: "Mmm, SWEET!"
- **Gag:** the third jump lands Tumble face first in a leaf pile with only his tail sticking out.
- **Moral:** Pip: "Don't say 'sour', ask a friend!"
- **Assets:** apple tree, stump, apple from Episode 2, new leaf pile.

### 3. The Crow and the Pitcher: "Pebble, Pebble, Plop!"

- **Hook:** Pip peering into a tall clay jug: "The water's way down there!"
- **Story:** On a hot day the water sits at the bottom of a tall jug. Pip's beak can't reach. Tumble pushes his paw in and it gets stuck (gag). Pip has an idea and drops pebbles in, one at a time, counting "One, two, three, four, five!" The water rises, plop, plop, to the top. Pip drinks, and the water now reaches Tumble's tongue too.
- **Gag:** Tumble's stuck paw comes out with a cork-pop sound and he tumbles backwards.
- **Bonus lesson:** counting 1 to 5.
- **Moral:** Pip: "Think it through, try something new!"
- **Assets:** new jug with a water level that rises (a flat disc inside the jug), pebbles, a hot-day light grade.

### 4. The Tortoise and the Hare: "Slow and Steady Bo"

- **Hook:** Tumble and Bo on a chalk start line, with Pip as referee holding a tiny flag. "Ready, steady..."
- **Story:** Tumble races Bo to the big stump. Tumble zooms ahead, looks back and shows off ("Too easy!"), then lies down for a "tiny rest" in the mushroom shade and starts snoring. Bo plods on, step by step, humming. Tumble wakes up just as Bo touches the stump. Tumble: "You won, Bo! Well done!" and they high-five.
- **Gag:** Tumble's snore makes a mushroom cap bob up and down. When he wakes he spins his legs in the air before he takes off.
- **Moral:** Bo: "Slow and steady gets there too!" Tumble's good-sport cheer is the second beat.
- **Assets:** Bo from Lulu & Bo, mushrooms, stump, start line and flag.

### 5. The Dog and His Reflection: "The Other Fox"

- **Hook:** Tumble on a log bridge staring down into the stream at "another fox" holding an apple. "Hey! Who's that?"
- **Story:** Tumble carries his apple across the log over the stream. In the water he sees a fox with an apple that looks bigger. He reaches for it, "I want THAT one!", and his own apple drops in, splash, and floats away. Pip offers his tiny berry and breaks it in half. Tumble laughs and they share. Tumble: "Your berry is the best!"
- **Gag:** Tumble makes a silly face at the reflection and it makes the same face back. Then a ripple turns the reflection wobbly.
- **Moral:** Pip: "Be happy with what you've got, and share it!"
- **Assets:** stream with a fake reflection (draw Tumble and the apple mirrored below the water line under a tinted, semi-transparent water plane, so no new shader is needed), log bridge, apple, berry.

### 6. The Ant and the Grasshopper: "Busy Little Ants"

- **Hook:** a line of tiny ants marching past Tumble's nose, each carrying a seed. "Where are they going?"
- **Story:** It's autumn. The ants work all day while Tumble plays in the leaves. "Come and play!" "Work first, play later!" Pip helps the ants carry one seed. The wind turns chilly and the first snowflake lands on Tumble's nose. He shivers, and his tummy rumbles. The ants open their little door and share a seed snack. The next day Tumble carries the biggest seed for them.
- **Gag:** Tumble tries to carry a seed the ant way, balanced on his head, and wobbles comically. The ants walk under him in a neat line.
- **Moral:** Tumble: "Help a little every day!"
- **Assets:** new ant model (low poly, instanced in a line), seeds, an anthill door, autumn grade, snowflake particles.

### 7. The North Wind and the Sun: "Scarf Off!"

- **Hook:** two faces in the sky, a puffy Wind cloud and a smiling Sun, both looking down at Tumble in a striped scarf. "Who can get the scarf off?"
- **Story:** Wind and Sun have a friendly contest, with Pip as referee and his whistle. Wind blows hard, WHOOSH, and Tumble only holds the scarf tighter while his ears flap. Pip is blown backwards and clings on to a flower. Sun shines softly and warmly, Tumble smiles, takes the scarf off and lies down in the sunshine. Sun wins, and Wind gives a gentle breeze to cool everyone.
- **Gag:** the gust spins Tumble round like a weathervane with his scarf streaming straight out.
- **Moral:** Pip: "Gentle and kind works best!"
- **Assets:** Wind and Sun faces as soft clay sky discs (the Episode 1 sky changes are a starting point), scarf as a cloth strip, whistle.
- **Note:** the brightness change from wind to sun must ramp over more than 0.5 s (no-flashing rule).

### 8. The Boy Who Cried Wolf: "Pip Who Cried Help"

- **Hook:** Pip hiding in a big flower and grinning: "Heeelp!"
- **Story:** Pip shouts "Help!" as a joke. Tumble rushes over, trips and lands in a puddle. Pip giggles: "Just joking!" It happens again. Then the picnic basket lid really does flop shut over Pip. "Help! Really!" Tumble stops: "Is it a joke?" He decides to check anyway and lifts the lid. Pip pops out, sorry and a bit wobbly: "Thank you. No more pretend help." Hug.
- **Gag:** Tumble's second rush ends with him sliding past the flower and spinning to a stop.
- **Softening:** no wolf and no danger. The "trouble" is a lid falling shut. The lesson is about trust and saying sorry.
- **Moral:** Pip: "Tell the truth, so friends know!"
- **Assets:** basket from Episode 2, big flower, puddle from Episode 1.

### 9. The Bundle of Sticks: "Stronger Together"

- **Hook:** Tumble holding one twig, and a big bundle tied with grass lying on the stump. "Can you snap the bundle?"
- **Story:** Tumble snaps one twig easily, snap! Then he tries the whole bundle: he pulls, pushes, sits on it and goes red in the face. It won't snap. Pip: "One stick is easy... lots of sticks together are strong!" Pip and Tumble use the bundle as a bridge across the stream that one twig never could be.
- **Gag:** Tumble sits on the bundle to snap it and it springs back and bounces him into the air.
- **Moral:** Pip: "Together we're strong!"
- **Assets:** twigs and a grass tie (cylinders), stump, stream.

### 10. The Dove and the Ant: "One Good Turn"

- **Hook:** a tiny ant spinning on a leaf in the stream. "Oh no, little ant!"
- **Story:** Pip spots an ant swept along the stream and drops a leaf. The ant climbs on and floats to the bank: "Thank you, Pip!" Later Pip lands in a sticky honey drip on a flower and can't lift his feet. The ant whistles and the whole ant line from Fable 6 marches up and pulls him free, heave-ho, pop!
- **Gag:** each tug stretches Pip like clay toffee before he pops loose and somersaults onto Tumble's nose.
- **Moral:** the ant: "Help a friend, and a friend helps you!"
- **Assets:** ants and leaf from Fable 6, stream, a honey drip (glossy shader preset), big flower from Fable 8.

## Song plan

One sound for the whole batch, built from three parts.

1. **Theme sting (2 s).** "Tumble and Pip!" Generated once and stored as stems (separate vocal and instrument tracks).
2. **Moral chorus (about 7 s).** Generated once with the best of 3 takes, then stored as stems and reused word for word:
   > Big or small, short or tall,
   > friends help friends, that's best of all!
3. **Fable verses (about 30 s each).** One generation per fable, using the chorus's tempo, key, instruments and singers. The verse carries the setup, the problem and the fix.

**Fingerprint (shared by the whole batch):** 100 BPM, 4/4 straight eighths, D major. Verses go D-G-A-D, problem bars go Bm-G-A, and the chorus goes D-A-Bm-G-D-A-D. Instruments: acoustic guitar lead, glockenspiel, upright bass and soft hand drum, with the Tumble and Pip leitmotifs. Genre: gentle storybook folk. Compared with the archive this differs on tempo, key, progression, lead instrument and form.

**Joining verse and chorus:** cut on a bar line. Use the existing `ENGINE.md` rule: only join where the instrumental chroma similarity is 0.9 or more. If a verse will not join cleanly, regenerate that verse with the chorus attached as the last section and keep the take whose chorus sounds closest to the stored one.

**Test first:** before producing all 8, make Fable 1 end to end and check the verse-to-chorus join by ear. This is the riskiest step in the plan.

## Production plan

### Dual-aspect staging

- Stage every scene so the action sits in a centre "safe core" that works in both 9:16 and 16:9.
- Each shot in `timeline.py` defines two cameras, `cam_short` and `cam_wide`. `render.py` takes `--aspect short|wide`.
- In wide framing, show both characters more often and use fewer extreme close-ups.
- Captions: the Short layout puts them in the bottom third. The wide layout puts them lower-centre in a smaller size, with the moral card unchanged.

### Cadence: 2 Shorts a day

The owner wants at least 2 Shorts a day. That fixes the production rate at **14 Shorts a week**, whatever the batch size, so the plan works backwards from it:

1. **Bank before launch.** Don't post until this whole batch (10 Shorts) and the episode are finished. Posting 2 a day from a buffer stops a slow day from breaking the streak.
2. **Batch 01 (10 Shorts) is the speed test.** Log hours and reviewer rounds per Short. If the pipeline holds 2 Shorts a day, from Batch 02 onward one batch is 14 Shorts a week and one episode of about 12 to 13 minutes.
3. **If it can't hold 2 a day,** post 1 a day rather than lower the quality gates or pad with re-cuts. Re-posting cut-down versions of the same Short is the kind of repetition YouTube's inauthentic content policy demonetises.

| Days | Work |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Theme sting, moral chorus (3 takes each), Bo's voice, two-camera support in `render.py` |
| 3-4 | Fable 1 end to end in both aspects, as the pipeline test. Owner review. |
| 5-9 | Fables 2-10, 2 per day, every gate in `ENGINE.md` Step 4 per Short |
| 10-11 | Episode: Story Stump intro, links, break, recap, outro, wide renders, assembly, reviewer pass on the full episode |
| 12 | Launch, with Batch 02 already in production |

### Publishing

- **Shorts:** two per day (morning and late afternoon UK time), in fable order. Fable 1 goes first because it teaches the chorus. The 10 Shorts run for 5 days.
- **Episode:** publish on day 3 so the later Shorts have a long-form video on the channel to send viewers to.
- **Settings:** Made for Kids, UK English.
- **Titles:** "Tiny Pip, Big Help! | Tumble & Pip Fables" for Shorts, "Fables About Friendship | Tumble & Pip | 10 Stories for Kids" for the episode.
- **Credit:** every description says "Based on Aesop's fable *The Lion and the Mouse*". It is honest, it is helpful for parents, and it shows the retelling is deliberate.

### Long-form ladder (towards hour-long videos)

Hour-long compilations do well for children's channels because they play on TVs for a long time, and each view adds a lot of watch time. They are only safe when they gather distinct episodes, not when they loop one clip set.

| Stage | When | Long-form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Batches 01-03 | one 9 to 13 minute episode per batch |
| 2 | after 4 batches | a 30 to 40 minute "Story Time" compilation of 3 or 4 episodes with a new opening, new links between episodes and one long sing-along |
| 3 | after 8 or more batches | a 60-minute compilation, plus a new chapter-style episode each month |

### What to measure (per Short and for the episode)

| Metric | Where | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| Viewed vs swiped away | YouTube Studio, Shorts | is the hook working? |
| Average percentage viewed | Shorts | does the story hold? |
| Subscribers gained per 1,000 views | Shorts | does the series pull people in? |
| Audience retention curve | Episode | where children drop off (which fable, the links, the recap) |
| Average view duration | Episode | the watch-hour route to monetisation |
